# Business Understanding

## 1. Business Overview

Company X is a real estate agency that acts as an intermediary between property owners or developer and prospective buyers or tenants. The company's primary source of income comes from commissions earned from successfully completed property transactions (*completed deals*).

In its operations, a single property can be marketed through multiple listings managed by different agents to increase market exposure and reach a wider pool of potential customers. Therefore, effective listing management is essential to improving property conversion opportunities, optimizing inventory efficiency, and maximizing company revenue.

## 2. Stakeholder

**Primary Stakeholder: Sales Manager**

The Sales Manager is responsible for overseeing sales performance, managing property inventory, monitoring agent productivity, and ensuring that sales activities support the company's business objectives.

## 3. Business Problem

A single property can be marketed through multiple listings managed by different agents. However, an increase in the number of listings does not necessarily lead to a proportional increase in successfully completed transactions.

This situation raises several business challenges:

1. **Conversion Performance:** Determine whether property and listing conversion rates meet the company's established performance targets.
2. **Inventory Efficiency:** Understand how effectively property and listing inventory is utilized and absorbed over time.
3. **Segment Performance:** Identify property segments with relatively low conversion rates.
4. **Conversion Drivers:** Evaluate the factors associated with differences in property conversion performance, including property characteristics and agent involvement.

## 4. Business Objectives

The primary business objective is to evaluate and improve the effectiveness of property and listing inventory management by increasing the proportion of inventory converted into successfully completed transactions.

The analysis uses the following conversion targets as business performance benchmarks.

### 4.1 Overall Targets

 Listing Conversion Rate : 40% 
 Property Conversion Rate : 58% 

### 4.2 Annual Targets

 Listing Conversion Rate : 30% 
 Property Conversion Rate : 35% 

The overall targets are used to evaluate cumulative conversion performance across the analysis period (2022–2024), while the annual targets are used to assess performance within each individual year.

## 5. Business Questions

The main business question is:

**How effectively is the company's property and listing inventory converted into successfully completed transactions?**

To address this question, the analysis is divided into three areas:

### A. Conversion and Inventory Performance

1. What are the company's property and listing conversion rates, and how do they compare with the established targets?
2. How do property and listing inventory absorption trends change each year?

### B. Conversion Drivers and Performance

3. Which property segments demonstrate the highest and lowest conversion performance?
4. What property characteristics and levels of agent involvement are associated with property conversion performance?

### C. Revenue and Commission Performance

5. Which property segments and locations generate the highest transaction value and company commission?

## 6. Business Question Answers

### A. Conversion and Inventory Performance

#### 6.1 What are the company's property and listing conversion rates, and how do they compare with the established targets?

The conversion analysis evaluates listing and property performance from two perspectives: overall performance across the entire analysis period (2022–2024) and annual performance for each individual year.

**Overall Conversion Performance**
 Listing Conversion Rate : 44%  (Above target)
 Property Conversion Rate : 58% (Above target)

The overall Listing Conversion Rate reached 44% out of 8,500 listings, exceeding the target of 40% by 4 percentage points.

Similarly, the overall Property Conversion Rate reached approximately 66% across 3,817 listed properties, exceeding the target of 58% by approximately 7.9 percentage points.

These results indicate that both conversion metrics exceeded their respective overall targets during the analysis period.

**Annual Conversion Performance**

Although overall conversion performance exceeded the established targets, the annual results show a declining trend throughout the three-year period.

2022 
    Listing Conversion Rate = 39% (Above Target) 
    Property Conversion Rate = 47% (Above Target)
2023 
    Listing Conversion Rate = 30% (Above Target)
    Property Conversion Rate = 40% (Abover Target)
2024 
    Listing Conversion Rate = 25% (Under Target)
    Property Conversion Rate = 38% (Above Target)

The annual Property Conversion Rate declined from 47% in 2022 to 38% in 2024. Despite this decrease, it remained above the annual target of 35% throughout the analysis period.

In contrast, the Listing Conversion Rate declined from 39% in 2022 to 25% in 2024. It met the annual target in 2023 but fell below the target in 2024.

Although the decline in conversion performance became smaller over time, the overall downward trend indicates that the company's ability to convert its inventory requires further attention.

**Factors Contributing to the Declining Annual Conversion Rates**

Several inventory-related factors may explain the decline in annual conversion performance:

1. **Increasing Available Inventory**

   Conversion rates are influenced by the amount of available inventory used as the denominator. As available listings and properties accumulate over time, the denominator increases. Consequently, even when the number of completed transactions grows annually, conversion rates may decline if transaction growth cannot keep pace with inventory expansion.

2. **Repeated Listings for the Same Property**

   Some properties are marketed through multiple listings without closing or deactivating their previous listings. As a result, new listings are added while older listings associated with the same property remain active, increasing the available listing inventory and potentially reducing the overall Listing Conversion Rate.

3. **Previously Sold Properties Returning to the Market**

   Some properties with completed transaction records reappear in the listing inventory. This may be related to rental transactions, where a property can be successfully rented and subsequently offered to the market again.

   As a result, the same property may contribute to the available inventory more than once over the analysis period, increasing the property inventory denominator and affecting the annual Property Conversion Rate.

**Business Implication:**

Although overall conversion performance exceeded the established targets, the declining annual trend highlights the importance of monitoring inventory accumulation and listing management.

The company should review repeated listings, distinguish between sales and rental transactions, and ensure that inventory records accurately reflect the current availability of each property.

#### 6.2 How do property and listing inventory absorption trends change each year?

A monthly breakdown was conducted to investigate fluctuations behind the annual conversion trends and identify the months contributing to changes in conversion performance.

The analysis uses a monthly conversion benchmark of 4% for both Listing Conversion Rate and Property Conversion Rate.

**2022**

Both listing and property conversion rates generally remained above the monthly target throughout the year, although their performance showed a gradual declining pattern.

**2023**

Listing conversion performance fluctuated throughout the year. Several months remained above the monthly target, but July recorded a conversion rate below the benchmark, potentially reflecting a decrease in completed sales during that period.

Property conversion followed a similar pattern of fluctuation but remained above the monthly target.

Another important observation was that the peak monthly conversion rates for both listings and properties declined compared with 2022. This may indicate that the increasing available inventory made it more difficult to maintain the previous year's conversion performance.

**2024**

Listing conversion performance weakened considerably. Only two months, April and August, recorded conversion rates above the monthly target, while most other months remained below the benchmark.

Property conversion also fluctuated throughout the year but generally remained above the monthly target.

**Business Implication:**

The monthly analysis highlights a weakening trend in listing conversion performance, particularly in 2024. The increasing available listing inventory may have contributed to this condition, as the growth in inventory was not matched by proportional growth in completed transactions.

The company should monitor monthly conversion performance alongside inventory growth and completed transactions to identify periods of declining sales effectiveness.

*Note: This monthly analysis evaluates conversion rate fluctuations rather than directly measuring inventory absorption. Inventory absorption requires a separate calculation based on the amount of inventory available during each period and the number of properties or listings successfully absorbed.*

### B. Conversion Drivers and Performance

#### 6.3 Which property segments demonstrate the highest and lowest conversion performance?

Conversion performance varies across cities, property types, listing channels, and price categories. Although the rankings may fluctuate from year to year, the following findings represent the overall results across the analysis period.

**City**

- **Highest conversion rate:** Barishal.
- **Lowest conversion rate:** Rangpur.
- Dhaka ranked third or fourth in conversion performance, depending on the metric. However, it recorded the highest revenue contribution and the largest number of properties sold.

This indicates that a high conversion rate does not necessarily translate into the highest transaction volume or revenue contribution.

**Property Type**

- Apartments demonstrated the strongest overall conversion performance.
- Commercial properties recorded the lowest Listing Conversion Rate.
- Houses recorded the lowest Property Conversion Rate.
- Land consistently ranked second in conversion performance.

**Listing Channel**

- All listing channels achieved the Listing Conversion Rate target.
- Partner listings recorded the highest Listing Conversion Rate.
- Exclusive listings recorded the lowest Listing Conversion Rate.
- However, none of the listing channels achieved the Property Conversion Rate target.

**Price Category**

- Cheap properties recorded the highest conversion performance.
- Medium-priced properties ranked second.
- Expensive properties recorded the lowest conversion performance and failed to meet the established conversion targets.

The same general ranking was observed for both listing and property conversion.

**Business Implication:**

Conversion performance differs considerably across business segments. The company should prioritize high-performing segments when evaluating future inventory opportunities while reviewing pricing, marketing, and sales strategies for underperforming segments.

Dhaka and apartments should also receive attention as important sales contributors because of their transaction volume and revenue contribution, even though their conversion rankings do not necessarily place them first.

#### 6.4 What property characteristics and levels of agent involvement are associated with property conversion performance?

The analysis identifies several differences in Property Conversion Rate across property characteristics.
The following rankings are based on the observed conversion rates, from highest to lowest.

Properties with one bathroom and one bedroom recorded the highest conversion rates within their respective categories. The zero-bathroom and zero-bedroom categories may include land properties, so these categories should be interpreted in the context of property type.
The analysis also considers agent involvement in property marketing. However, the specific relationship between the number of agents assigned to a property and its conversion performance requires further examination before drawing a definitive conclusion.

**Business Implication:**

Property conversion should not be evaluated solely based on physical property characteristics or the number of agents assigned. Other factors, such as pricing, location, market demand, listing quality, and sales execution, should also be considered.

These findings indicate observed associations rather than direct causal relationships.

### C. Revenue and Commission Performance

#### 6.5 Which property segments and locations generate the highest transaction value and company commission?

The analysis shows that revenue and commission contributions vary across cities and property types.

**City**

- **Highest contribution:** Dhaka.
- **Lowest contribution:** Barishal.

Dhaka recorded the highest revenue contribution and the largest number of completed property sales, while Barishal recorded the lowest contribution.

**Property Type**

- **Highest contribution:** Apartments.
- **Lowest contribution:** Land.

Apartments generated the highest revenue contribution, while land recorded the lowest contribution.

**Transaction Volume**
Property Type : Highest(Apartment), Lowest(Commercial)
City :Highets(Dhaka), Lowest(Barishal)

**Business Implication:**

Dhaka and apartments represent important contributors to the company's transaction performance. These segments may provide opportunities for targeted sales strategies and future inventory acquisition.

However, transaction value, company revenue, and commission should be assessed separately. A high transaction value does not automatically result in the highest commission because commission rates and transaction conditions may vary.

The company should therefore evaluate actual commission amounts alongside transaction value and transaction volume when determining the most profitable segments.

## 7. Overall Business Insights

The analysis highlights several important findings for the Sales Manager:

1. **Overall conversion performance exceeded the established targets.** Both Listing Conversion Rate and Property Conversion Rate achieved their overall benchmarks. However, annual conversion performance showed a declining trend.
2. **Listing conversion requires particular attention.** The annual Listing Conversion Rate declined to 25% in 2024, falling below the 30% target.
3. **Inventory accumulation may affect conversion performance.** Repeated listings and properties returning to the market can increase available inventory without generating proportional growth in completed transactions.
4. **Conversion performance varies across business segments.** Location, property type, listing channel, and price category show different conversion patterns.
5. **Property characteristics provide additional insights into conversion performance.** However, these relationships should be interpreted as associations rather than evidence of causation.
6. **Dhaka and apartments are major transaction contributors.** These segments deserve attention when developing future sales and inventory strategies.

Overall, the company should focus not only on expanding its inventory but also on improving inventory quality, controlling duplicate or repeated listings, and developing segment-based sales strategies to increase the conversion of properties into successfully completed transactions.
