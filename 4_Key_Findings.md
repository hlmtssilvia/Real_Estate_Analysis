# Key Findings

## 1. Inventory Growth Is Not Matched by Proportional Conversion Growth
The total number of listings increased significantly from 2,879 in 2022 to 5,318 in 2024, representing approximately 84.7% growth. Meanwhile, completed listings increased only from 1,121 to 1,337 over the same period, representing approximately 19.3% growth.
This difference indicates that the increase in inventory has not been accompanied by a proportional increase in listings successfully converted into completed transactions.
**Business Interpretation**
Inventory growth significantly outpaced the growth of completed listings, indicating a gap between the company's increasing supply and its ability to convert inventory into transactions.
This condition may lead to an increasing number of unconverted listings, particularly if new listings continue to be added without effectively managing existing inventory.
**Business Implications**
- Inventory growth without a proportional increase in conversion may create additional management workloads and potentially increase operational costs.
- A growing number of unconverted listings may increase the risk of inventory accumulation and make conversion targets more difficult to achieve.
- Multiple listings for the same property may increase management complexity if ineffective or outdated listings are not regularly reviewed and updated.

## 2. Carry-over Inventory Continues to Accumulate, but Its Contribution to Completed Listings Remains Limited
Based on the composition of available listings by inventory source, the proportion of carry-over listings increased each year. In 2024, nearly 50% of all available listings originated from carry-over inventory.
However, the contribution of carry-over inventory to completed listings continued to decline. In 2024, only approximately 10% of all completed listings originated from carry-over inventory.
**Business Interpretation**
There is a gap between the contribution of carry-over inventory to listing availability and its contribution to completed listings.
This indicates that the company converts a relatively larger proportion of newly acquired listings compared to listings carried over from previous periods. Older inventory may face greater challenges in converting into completed transactions.
However, this difference in composition does not directly prove that carry-over listings have a lower conversion rate. A comparison of conversion rates by inventory source is required to confirm this.
**Business Implications**
- The company risks continuously adding new inventory while existing inventory remains unresolved.
- Carry-over listings require specific evaluation based on listing age, property pricing, property condition, and marketing activity history.
- New inventory acquisition should consider the company's capacity to manage and convert existing inventory.

## 3. Conversion Rates Continue to Decline, with Listing Conversion Falling Further Below Target
Both conversion indicators showed a declining trend throughout the 2022–2024 period.
Listing Conversion Rate : 39%, 30%, 25% 
Property Conversion Rate : 47%, 40%, 38% 
The Property Conversion Rate declined every year but remained above the annual target of 35%. Meanwhile, the Listing Conversion Rate met its target in 2023 but dropped to 25% in 2024, falling below the annual target of 30%. Although the rate of decline in both indicators has started to slow, the trend still requires attention because it does not yet indicate a recovery in conversion performance.
**Business Interpretation**
The declining conversion rates indicate that the company's ability to convert inventory has not developed in line with inventory availability.
The gap in target achievement between Property Conversion Rate and Listing Conversion Rate also suggests that conversion performance at the listing level faces greater challenges.
One area requiring further evaluation is listing management, particularly because a single property can have multiple listings. The growth in listing volume without a proportional increase in completed transactions may indicate inefficiencies in inventory management.
Additionally, the growth of new inventory and the accumulation of carry-over inventory may increase the volume of inventory that needs to be managed and affect conversion rate calculations.
**Business Implications**
- Listing Conversion Rate should become a key evaluation priority because its performance fell below the annual target in 2024.
- The company should evaluate listing management effectiveness, including repeated listings, long-standing inactive listings, and listings that have not generated transactions.
- The Property Conversion Rate, which remains above target, should be maintained while improving listing conversion performance.
## 4. Conversion Performance and Sales Contributions Vary Across Property Segments
The analysis reveals differences in performance across location, property type, and price category.
**By City:**
- Barishal recorded the highest conversion rate, while Rangpur recorded the lowest.
- Dhaka was the largest contributor to the number of properties sold and total revenue.
- Despite having the largest sales and revenue contributions, Dhaka did not record the highest conversion rate.
**By Property Type:**
- Apartments dominated property share and demonstrated strong conversion performance and revenue contribution.
- Commercial properties recorded the lowest Listing Conversion Rate.
- Houses recorded the lowest Property Conversion Rate.
**By Price Category:**
- Properties in the Cheap category recorded the highest conversion rates at both listing and property levels.
- The Cheap category also accounted for the largest share of property inventory compared to other price categories.
**Business Interpretation**
These differences indicate that sales performance is not determined solely by inventory volume but also by property characteristics and segmentation.
Dhaka has the largest contribution to transactions and revenue because it accounts for a dominant share of the company's inventory. However, a larger inventory volume does not automatically translate into the highest conversion rate.
Meanwhile, Barishal demonstrates relatively high conversion efficiency despite contributing a smaller share of total sales and revenue.
At the property type and price category levels, apartments and Cheap-category properties demonstrate relatively stronger performance. In contrast, houses and commercial properties require further evaluation based on their respective conversion weaknesses.
**Business Implications**
- The company should avoid applying the same marketing and inventory management strategies across all cities and property types.
- Sales strategies should be tailored to the characteristics of each segment, including conversion performance, inventory volume, and revenue contribution.
- Low-conversion segments should be evaluated to identify sales barriers and determine appropriate improvement strategies.

## 5. Affordable Apartments Represent One of the Company's Core Inventory Strengths
Apartments and properties in the Cheap price category demonstrate relatively high conversion rates at both listing and property levels.
Additionally, both categories account for a dominant share of the company's inventory compared to other segments.
**Business Interpretation**
The combination of apartment property type and affordable pricing represents one of the company's potentially strongest inventory segments.
The relatively high conversion rates indicate that this segment performs well compared to other categories.
This may suggest that affordable apartment offerings align well with the demand in the market served by the company.
However, high conversion rates and sales volume do not necessarily indicate the highest profitability. Transaction values and commission contributions should also be considered when determining business priorities.
**Business Implications**
- The company can maintain the supply of affordable apartments as one of its priority inventory segments.
- Sales strategies for this strong-performing segment should be maintained and evaluated regularly.
- The company should continue developing strategies for other property segments to avoid excessive dependence on a single property type and price category combination.

## 6. Agent Workload Does Not Show a Consistent Relationship with Conversion Performance
The analysis of the number of listings handled by agents and their conversion performance does not reveal a consistent pattern indicating that handling more listings leads to higher conversion rates.
Therefore, increasing the number of listings managed by an agent cannot automatically be considered an indicator of improved sales effectiveness.
**Business Interpretation**
The number of listings handled does not necessarily reflect an agent's effectiveness in converting inventory into completed transactions.
Conversion performance may also be associated with other factors, such as inventory quality, property characteristics, marketing strategies, and agents' ability to manage potential buyers or tenants.
Therefore, agent performance evaluation should not rely solely on the number of listings managed but should also consider conversion outcomes and the characteristics of the assigned inventory.
**Business Implications**
- Agent performance evaluations should consider conversion rates and completed transactions, not just the number of listings managed.
- Inventory allocation should take into account agents' capacity and the characteristics of the assigned inventory.
- The company can further investigate the factors that differentiate high-converting agents from those with lower conversion performance.

## 7. Having More Listings per Property Does Not Guarantee Faster Conversion
The analysis indicates that properties requiring more listings before being successfully converted do not necessarily experience faster conversion.
A higher number of listings associated with a single property also cannot be used as a direct indicator of a higher likelihood of conversion.
**Business Interpretation**
Using multiple listings for a single property can be a strategy to expand marketing reach. However, simply increasing the number of listings does not necessarily resolve the underlying challenges that prevent a property from being converted.
Properties requiring multiple listings before being successfully sold may indicate challenges in the marketing process, such as inappropriate pricing, limited property attractiveness, incomplete listing information, or ineffective marketing strategies.
However, the number of listings may also reflect the company's marketing strategy. Therefore, the relationship between listing volume and conversion speed should not be interpreted as a direct cause-and-effect relationship.
**Business Implications**
- The company should evaluate property conditions and pricing before creating additional listings for the same property.
- Listing quality should be improved by ensuring complete information, relevant photos, clear descriptions, and pricing that reflects market conditions.
- The performance of multiple listings associated with the same property should be monitored to ensure that additional listings provide meaningful value to marketing activities.

## 8. Revenue Concentration in Dhaka and Apartments Highlights Segment-Based Growth Opportunities
Dhaka was the largest contributor to total revenue and transaction volume by location. Meanwhile, apartments were the leading property type in terms of revenue contribution and transaction volume.
On the other hand, Barishal and several other cities recorded relatively high conversion rates but contributed a smaller share of total revenue and properties sold compared to Dhaka.
**Business Interpretation**
The company's revenue and transaction contributions remain concentrated in specific locations and property types, particularly Dhaka and apartments.
Meanwhile, cities with high conversion rates but relatively lower sales contributions may be considered candidates for further evaluation as potential growth opportunities.
The differences between conversion rates and revenue contributions indicate that expansion strategies should not rely solely on conversion efficiency. The company should also consider inventory size, average transaction value, market demand potential, and commission contributions.
**Business Implications**
- The company should maintain the performance of segments that contribute the largest share of revenue and transactions, particularly Dhaka and apartments.
- Cities with high conversion rates but relatively low revenue contributions can be considered for gradual expansion strategy testing.
- Expansion opportunities should be evaluated based on transaction value, commission, inventory availability, and market potential before increasing inventory supply.
